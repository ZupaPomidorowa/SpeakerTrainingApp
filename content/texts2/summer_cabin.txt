In this region, summer is all about retreating to a countryside timber dwelling. Here are some of the best, from a mid-Century home to a modern cabin overlooking the ocean.

The Nordic people tend to stay close to home in the summer. And why not, when the annual ritual is to get away from it all by spending the whole summer at their rural retreat?

These summer houses are all about dipping into a simpler, more traditional lifestyle. Originally, they were back-to-nature basic. A prolific summer cabin designer, Norwegian mid-20th-Century architect Wenche Selmer believed they were a metaphor for a simple, good life. "What can you live without?" she often asked her clients.

In recent times, these cabins and summerhouses – some 1.8 million of them across the Nordics – have been souped up with modern conveniences, as the demand has increased for comfort. Now environmental concerns combine with a desire for reduced consumption and a digital detox, producing a simpler aesthetic. Whatever their spec, they allow people to embrace friluftsliv, the Norwegian concept of open-air living.
